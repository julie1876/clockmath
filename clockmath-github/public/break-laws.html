Break laws at a glance

  • 21 states require a meal or lunch break for at least some adult workers.
  • 8 states require paid rest breaks (usually 10 minutes for every 4 hours worked).
  • The other states have no break law for adults, so federal rules apply.

What federal law says (applies in every state)

  • Federal law doesn't require employers to give meal or rest breaks.
  • If your employer does give short breaks (usually 5 to 20 minutes), they must be paid.
  • Meal breaks of 30 minutes or more can be unpaid, but only if you're fully relieved of work. If you have to keep working while you eat, that time must be paid.
  • Most employers must give reasonable break time and a private space (not a bathroom) for nursing employees to pump breast milk for up to a year after a baby's birth.

Frequently asked questions

Which states require a lunch break?

California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Illinois, Kentucky, Maine, Maryland (retail only), Massachusetts, Minnesota, Nebraska (some industries), Nevada, New Hampshire, New York, North Dakota, Oregon, Rhode Island, Tennessee, Vermont, Washington and West Virginia have meal break rules for at least some adult workers.

Which states require paid rest breaks?

California, Colorado, Kentucky, Minnesota, Nevada, Oregon and Washington require paid rest breaks for most workers, and Vermont requires reasonable restroom breaks. Illinois requires them only for some hotel workers.

Does federal law require breaks?

No. Federal law doesn't require meal or rest breaks. But short breaks of about 5 to 20 minutes must be paid if your employer gives them, and meal breaks can only be unpaid if you're fully relieved of work.

Do these rules apply to teenagers?

Not always. Many states have extra break rules for workers under 18. This page covers adult employees.
