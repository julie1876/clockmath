Nevada Break Laws: Meal and Rest Break Rules

Nevada break law in short: Meal break: 30 min for 8 hr shifts. Rest breaks: Paid 10 min per 4 hrs worked.

Meal and lunch breaks in Nevada

Employees working 8 continuous hours must get a 30-minute meal period.

Rest breaks in Nevada

Employees get a paid 10-minute rest break for every 4 hours worked (or major fraction), ideally in the middle of each work period. Not required if the workday is under 3½ hours.

Good to know

  • Applies to employers with 2 or more employees. Union-covered employees are excluded, and the Labor Commissioner can grant exemptions.

What federal law says (applies in every state)

  • Federal law doesn't require employers to give meal or rest breaks.
  • If your employer does give short breaks (usually 5 to 20 minutes), they must be paid.
  • Meal breaks of 30 minutes or more can be unpaid, but only if you're fully relieved of work. If you have to keep working while you eat, that time must be paid.
  • Most employers must give reasonable break time and a private space (not a bathroom) for nursing employees to pump breast milk for up to a year after a baby's birth.

Frequently asked questions

Do I get a lunch break in Nevada?

Are breaks paid in Nevada?

Yes. Employees get a paid 10-minute rest break for every 4 hours worked (or major fraction), ideally in the middle of each work period. Not required if the workday is under 3½ hours.

Can my employer make me work 8 hours without a break in Nevada?

It depends on how long your shift is. In Nevada: 30 min for 8 hr shifts. Shorter shifts may not require a break.
