Maine Break Laws: Meal and Rest Break Rules

Maine break law in short: Meal break: 30 min after 6 hrs. No required paid rest breaks.

Meal and lunch breaks in Maine

Employees must be given a chance to take at least 30 consecutive minutes of rest after 6 consecutive hours of work, except in emergencies. It can be used to eat.

Rest breaks in Maine

Maine doesn't require paid rest breaks for adults. If your employer gives short breaks (usually 5 to 20 minutes), federal law says they must be paid.

Good to know

  • This doesn't apply where fewer than 3 employees are on duty and the work allows frequent breaks, or where a union contract or written agreement says otherwise.

What federal law says (applies in every state)

  • Federal law doesn't require employers to give meal or rest breaks.
  • If your employer does give short breaks (usually 5 to 20 minutes), they must be paid.
  • Meal breaks of 30 minutes or more can be unpaid, but only if you're fully relieved of work. If you have to keep working while you eat, that time must be paid.
  • Most employers must give reasonable break time and a private space (not a bathroom) for nursing employees to pump breast milk for up to a year after a baby's birth.

Frequently asked questions

Do I get a lunch break in Maine?

Yes. Employees must be given a chance to take at least 30 consecutive minutes of rest after 6 consecutive hours of work, except in emergencies. It can be used to eat.

Are breaks paid in Maine?

Maine doesn't require rest breaks for adults. But if your employer gives you a short break of about 5 to 20 minutes, federal law says it must be paid. Meal breaks of 30 minutes or more can be unpaid if you're fully relieved of work.

Can my employer make me work 8 hours without a break in Maine?

It depends on how long your shift is. In Maine: 30 min after 6 hrs. Shorter shifts may not require a break.
