New Mexico Break Laws: No Required Breaks for Adults

New Mexico doesn't have a state law requiring meal or rest breaks for adult employees. Federal law applies instead: breaks aren't required, but short breaks your employer gives must be paid.

Meal and lunch breaks in New Mexico

New Mexico doesn't require employers to give adult employees a meal or lunch break. If your employer offers one that's 30 minutes or longer and you're fully relieved of work, it can be unpaid.

Rest breaks in New Mexico

New Mexico doesn't require paid rest breaks for adults. If your employer gives short breaks (usually 5 to 20 minutes), federal law says they must be paid.

What federal law says (applies in every state)

  • Federal law doesn't require employers to give meal or rest breaks.
  • If your employer does give short breaks (usually 5 to 20 minutes), they must be paid.
  • Meal breaks of 30 minutes or more can be unpaid, but only if you're fully relieved of work. If you have to keep working while you eat, that time must be paid.
  • Most employers must give reasonable break time and a private space (not a bathroom) for nursing employees to pump breast milk for up to a year after a baby's birth.

Frequently asked questions

Do I get a lunch break in New Mexico?

No. New Mexico law doesn't require a lunch break for adult employees, so it's up to your employer's policy. Many employers still offer one.

Are breaks paid in New Mexico?

New Mexico doesn't require rest breaks for adults. But if your employer gives you a short break of about 5 to 20 minutes, federal law says it must be paid. Meal breaks of 30 minutes or more can be unpaid if you're fully relieved of work.

Can my employer make me work 8 hours without a break in New Mexico?

Yes, for adults. New Mexico has no state law requiring breaks, so an employer can legally schedule an 8-hour shift without one, unless a union contract or company policy says otherwise.
